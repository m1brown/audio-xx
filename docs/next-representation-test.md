# Next representation test — evidence with no axis

*Recorded 2026-08-19. Not scheduled. No new proposition type in the pass that found it.*

## The observation

SoundStage! on the Acora QRC-2, already admitted and stored:

> The QRC‑2 delivered faster transients, greater dynamic range, stronger realism
> of instrumental tone and better bass clarity, without compression even at very
> high levels.
>
> Condition: side-by-side comparison in a high-end store showroom with both the
> QRC‑2 and MRC‑2 under the same conditions.

## Why it matters

This is the *better* of the two published observations for the question Nathan
actually asked, and it is the one the assessment cannot use.

"No compression even at very high levels" is a property of the loudspeaker under
level. Unlike the Stereophile listening note — made through Ideon sources and JMF
electronics, and therefore transfer-limited — this finding does not belong to the
partnering equipment. It would transfer to Nathan's chain, and it bears directly
on a system whose amplifier delivers 200 watts into the load the speaker presents.

## Why it is unreachable

It carries no `axis` and no `direction`. `toAttributeRecords` emits nothing for
it, so it never becomes a premise, so no relation can rest on it and no sentence
can be licensed from it. Stored, admitted, structurally invisible.

The premise layer can only see claims that have already been reduced to a
position on one of the four tonal axes. Dynamic behaviour under level is not a
position on an axis, and forcing it onto one would be the same category error
that flattened `100 W @ 8Ω` and `4Ω` into `power_load` and licensed a drive
conclusion the evidence never established.

## The test this sets

Transferability is a property of CLAIM CONTENT, not of condition kind. The
current model derives it from the condition alone (`associated_equipment` →
transfer_limited, everything else → conditioned), which is right for the
Stereophile case and says nothing about this one.

Two questions to answer before any code:

1. Can an observation about behaviour-under-load participate in reasoning
   without inventing an axis for it to sit on?
2. Is the missing thing a proposition type, or a second premise SHAPE alongside
   the axis position and the typed quantity?

Answer them from real observations. The typed-quantity work earned its keep
because it started from one published string that produced one false sentence.
