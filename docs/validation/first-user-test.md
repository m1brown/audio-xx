# Audio XX — First-User Validation Package

Purpose: learn whether serious audiophiles **understand, trust, complete, and value** Audio XX. Not a design-opinion session. One moderator (Mike), one tester, 20 minutes, screen shared, tester drives.

Ground rules for the moderator: never explain the product, never defend a response, never fill silence. When they stall, say "what are you thinking?" and nothing else. The product must survive on its own.

---

## 1. Moderated test script (20 minutes)

**0:00–2:00 — Setup**
"Thanks for doing this. I'm testing a website, not you — nothing you do is wrong. Please think out loud the whole time: what you're reading, what you expect, what annoys you. I won't help or explain, because real visitors won't have me next to them. Please share your screen and open audio-xx.com — but don't click anything yet."

**2:00–4:00 — Cover read (understanding)**
With the homepage on screen, before any interaction:
- "In your own words — what is this site, and who is it for?"
- "What do you think happens if you write in that box?"
- "Would you have stayed on this page if you'd landed here from a forum link? Why?"

**4:00–11:00 — Task 1: their real system (completion + trust)**
"You own a hi-fi system. Ask this site to assess it, exactly the way you'd naturally type it. Take it wherever you want."
Do not correct their phrasing. Let them read the full assessment in silence. Then:
- "Read me one line you agree with and one line you disagree with or doubt."
- "Did it tell you anything you didn't already know or hadn't put into words?"
- "Would you act on its main suggestion? Why or why not?"

**11:00–15:00 — Task 2: their real question (value)**
"What's the actual audio question on your mind right now — next purchase, an upgrade you're debating, something bugging you? Ask it."
Let them follow up at least once if they want to. Then:
- "Is that answer usable — could you do something with it today?"

**15:00–17:00 — The alternative (value anchor)**
- "Where would you normally have taken those two questions — forums, reviewers, a dealer, ChatGPT?"
- "How does what you just got compare with what you'd get there — better, worse, different how?"

**17:00–20:00 — Close (value + return)**
- "Will you come back to this site on your own in the next month? For what?"
- "Who do you know that you would — or specifically would not — send this to?"
- "If this cost something — say the price of a magazine subscription — what would it have to do to be worth it?"
- "One thing that, if unfixed, would make you never return?"

## 2. Five observation questions

The moderator answers these from *watching*, not from asking:

1. **Understood?** Within the first 30 seconds on the cover, did they correctly say what Audio XX is and does — without prompting?
2. **Completed?** Did they get from typing their system to a full assessment without help, backtracking, or confusion about what to do next?
3. **Read or skimmed?** Did they actually read the assessment — and at which section did their attention stop?
4. **Trust break?** Was there any moment of visible trust loss — a frown, a laugh, "that's just wrong," a wrong product, a price that made them flinch? Capture the exact trigger.
5. **Pulled back in?** Did they ask a second question *unprompted* — the single strongest behavioral signal of value?

## 4. Invitation message

Subject: **20 minutes to test something built for people like you?**

> Hi ___,
>
> I've built something I'd like your judgment on: Audio XX (audio-xx.com), a site that writes a considered assessment of a hi-fi system — how the components work together, where the bottleneck is, whether anything should change.
>
> I need 20 minutes on a video call with someone who actually owns a serious system and has strong opinions about gear — which is why I'm asking you. You'd share your screen, use the site on your own equipment, and tell me exactly what you think, including where it's wrong. Blunt is useful; polite is not.
>
> Nothing to prepare, nothing to buy, and I'm not selling you anything — I'm finding out whether this is worth an audiophile's time before I put it in front of strangers.
>
> Any 20-minute slot this week works. Interested?
>
> — Mike

## Session logistics (for Mike)

- 5 sessions is enough for this round; patterns repeat after 3–4.
- Recruit across the spread: at least one veteran with expensive gear, one mid-tier enthusiast, one relative beginner with a real system.
- Record the call (with permission) so notes can stay light during the session.
- Fill in the scorecard within 10 minutes of each session ending, while reactions are fresh.
- The decision after 5 sessions is one question: *did a majority understand, complete, trust, and say they'd return?* Each "no" column points at the next phase of work — from evidence, not benchmarks.
